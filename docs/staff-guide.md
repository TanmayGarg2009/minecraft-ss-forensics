# Staff Guide: Reviewing Forensic Reports in the Standalone Viewer

This guide provides Minecraft server staff, anti-cheat analysts, and tournament referees with procedures for evaluating forensic reports generated by players.

---

## 1. Opening the Staff Report Viewer

1. Launch `MinecraftSsForensics.exe` on your Windows PC.
2. In the top navigation bar, click the **"Staff Report Viewer"** tab.
3. Click **"Open Report File (.json)"** and select the `.json` file provided by the player.

---

## 2. Verifying Report Cryptographic Integrity

Upon loading, the viewer automatically inspects the embedded SHA-256 seal:

- **Green Badge: "CRYPTOGRAPHIC SEAL VERIFIED"**: Confirms that the report data is authentic and has not been edited or tampered with since the scanner generated it.
- **Red Alert: "TAMPER DETECTED / SEAL MISMATCH"**: Indicates that finding records, timestamps, mod inventory entries, or summary counters were modified in a text editor. Treat tampered reports with extreme skepticism.

---

## 3. The 5-Second Investigation Overview

The top of the viewer presents an instant executive summary:

- **Executive Verdict**: Plain-language determination (`CONFIRMED CHEATS DETECTED`, `POLICY VIOLATION`, `SUSPICIOUS INDICATORS`, or `NO EVIDENCE DETECTED`).
- **Summary Metrics**:
  - `Confirmed Cheats`: Definite cheat binaries, classes, or prohibited tools.
  - `Strong Indicators`: Circumstantial traces like BAM/Prefetch execution of known cheat utilities.
  - `Prohibited Mods`: Authentic mods that violate the server's specific gamemode ruleset.
  - `Needs Review`: Unknown or local modifications requiring human discretion.
  - `Informational`: Harmless occurrences (e.g. renamed official mods).

---

## 4. Deep-Dive Forensic Inspection

### A. Categorized Findings & Evidentiary Standard
Select any finding in the findings list to review its complete forensic sheet:
- **Technical Explanation**: Exactly what raw telemetry was observed.
- **Why It Matters**: The security and anti-cheat relevance.
- **Alternative Legitimate Explanations**: Alternative hypotheses (e.g., screen recording overlays, graphics hooks, or modpack renames).
- **What Is Proven vs. What Is Not Proven**: Explicit boundaries on what the evidence technically demonstrates versus what remains unproven.

### B. Mod Inventory & Authenticity vs. Policy
- Navigate to the **"Mod Inventory"** tab.
- Notice the dual classification for each mod:
  1. **Authenticity State**: Cryptographically verified against Modrinth/CurseForge (`VERIFIED_EXACT_RELEASE`, `HASH_MISMATCH`, `UNKNOWN`).
  2. **Gameplay Policy**: Allowed, Prohibited, or Restricted under the server's ruleset.
- *Example*: A player using Freecam may have a verified authentic Modrinth download (`VERIFIED_EXACT_RELEASE`), but it is classified as `PROHIBITED` under Crystal PvP rules.

### C. Process Memory & Module Provenance
- Navigate to the **"Process & Modules"** tab to view the active JVM environment.
- Check the **Provenance Assessment** column:
  - Modules extracted by JNA, LWJGL, or OpenAL are marked `Legitimate JNA native stub extraction`.
  - Unsigned non-system DLLs in temporary directories without provenance are flagged for review.

### D. Execution History & Timestamps
- Navigate to the **"Execution History"** tab to view historical traces from Prefetch, BAM, and PCA.
- Cross-reference the **Last Execution Time** with the match timestamp or the player's session log to determine if a suspected utility was run during the match or days prior.

### E. USB Removable Storage Devices
- Review historically connected flash drives under the **"USB Devices"** tab.
- Useful for detecting players who launch ghost clients from a USB drive and unplug it before screenshare.

---

## 5. Offline HTML Report Export & Sharing

Click **"Preview in Browser"** or **"Export Interactive HTML"**:
- Generates a standalone, beautiful HTML5 dashboard with zero external CSS/JS dependencies.
- Can be archived in your staff records, attached to ban appeal tickets, or shared with senior server administrators.
